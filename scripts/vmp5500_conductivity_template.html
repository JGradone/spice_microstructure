VMP5500 conductivity: a slow sensor response explains the salty bias; metre-scale noise remains

Sea-Bird SBE4 conductivity cell #{{n:sbe4.serial}} (cal {{n:sbe4.cal_date}}) with SBE3 thermometer #{{n:sbe3.serial}} (cal {{n:sbe3.cal_date}}), unpumped, on a free-falling VMP-5500. Compared with the VMP-250IR's JFE (JAC) CT sensor and the ship's SBE 911 CTD.

Bottom line. Across {{n:profiles.vmp5500}} VMP5500 casts, salinity from the SBE4 is about {{n:noise_ratio}}× noisier than the VMP250's at 1 m scale ({{n:noise_median.vmp5500}} vs {{n:noise_median.vmp250}} g/kg) and reads +{{n:offset_median.vmp5500}} g/kg salty of the ship CTD (median; the VMP250 is +{{n:offset_median.vmp250}}). Both get worse after about 24 August.

The salty bias is explained by response time. Measured against the nose FP07, the SBE4 behaves like a first-order sensor with a time constant of about {{n:response.sbe4_tau_median}} s (about 5 m of fall), and the SBE3 about {{n:response.sbe3_tau_median}} s. A slow cell descending through water whose conductivity keeps falling reads high. Modelling that lag removes the bias, and the bias grows late in the cruise because those casts crossed steeper gradients, with the same lag.

The metre-scale noise is not explained. Timing, response matching, thermal-mass correction and despiking all leave it in place, and it is what makes VMP5500 salinity unusable for gradients. Calibration coefficients and the temperature channel's sampling are fine. We would like a second opinion on both.

How the comparison works

A ship CTD cast lies within 15 km and 6 h of 42 of the 45 VMP5500 casts. Casts hours apart see the thermocline heaved up and down by internal waves, so comparing at the same depth mixes real displacement into the difference. The quantitative comparisons are therefore made along the temperature–salinity curve: VMP salinity is compared with the ship CTD's salinity at the same conservative temperature Θ, which heave does not change. VMP5500 CT data are placed at the CT sensor's own depth, 1.57 m above the profiler's pressure sensor; this affects the depth panels only, not the T–S comparisons or the salinity values.

What the data show

Slow sensor response explains the offset

The FP07 thermistors on the nose respond in milliseconds and sit {{n:response.separation_m}} m below the Sea-Bird sensors (0.30 m below the pressure sensor, which is 1.57 m below the CT pair). Delaying the FP07 temperature by that distance gives the true temperature at the CT sensors, and with the ship CTD's T–S relation, the conductivity an ideal cell would read. Each Sea-Bird channel is then fitted as a delay plus a first-order lag of its ideal signal ({{n:response.casts}} casts, 100–500 dbar).

Questions for you

  1. Is a time constant of about {{n:response.sbe4_tau_median}} s plausible for an unpumped SBE4 on a VMP-5500 falling at 0.8 m/s, and is there a duct or pump arrangement that would explain it? The SBE3 at about {{n:response.sbe3_tau_median}} s is also far slower than its pumped specification.
  2. What could cause metre-scale conductivity noise of about 0.07 g/kg in salinity that no timing or response correction removes? Could flushing that varies with the flow around the instrument produce it?
  3. Would you recommend a post-cruise calibration of SBE4 #4076, a flow or flushing check, or another test before we decide whether the sensor or its mounting is at fault?

Method notes

  • Profiles processed with microstructure-tpw; salinity and Θ with TEOS-10 (gsw). VMP data binned to 1 m in depth.
  • Noise: standard deviation of 1 m salinity minus its 9 m running median, 150–500 m. Offset: median of VMP salinity minus ship CTD salinity at the same Θ, using the ship cast's T–S relation (median SA in 0.05 °C bins).
  • Ship CTD: SBE 911, sensor pair 1, nearest cast within 15 km and 6 h. N²: TEOS-10 from linear fits of Θ and SA over the stated scale.
  • VMP250: {{n:profiles.vmp250}} casts reaching 200 m. The figures and numbers come from scripts/diagnose_vmp5500_conductivity.py in the processing repository.
